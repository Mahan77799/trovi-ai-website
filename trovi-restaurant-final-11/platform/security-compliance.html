Canadian privacy principles

Collect less. Explain why. Protect what you keep.

The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada identifies accountability, purpose, consent, limiting collection, limiting use and retention, safeguards, openness and access among PIPEDA’s core principles.

01

Identify the purpose

Know why the information is being collected before or at the time of collection.

02

Meaningful consent

People should understand what is being collected, why it is needed and how it will be used.

03

Limit collection

Collect only what is needed for the purpose instead of turning every call into a data grab.

04

Use appropriate safeguards

Safeguards should reflect the sensitivity of the information and the risks around it.

If calls are recorded

Tell callers what is happening and why.

Canada’s Privacy Commissioner says organizations subject to PIPEDA should inform customers that a call is being recorded, clearly state the purpose and seek consent. The guidance also emphasizes limiting use and retention.

Trovi setup principle: recording should be a deliberate business decision, not an invisible default. The exact notice and process should match the purpose and legal obligations of the business using it.

Healthcare note

Health information raises the bar.

Ontario’s PHIPA framework limits the collection, use and disclosure of personal health information and emphasizes collecting no more than is reasonably necessary. Dental and other healthcare deployments should be reviewed with those obligations in mind.

This page is product information, not legal advice. Privacy requirements can vary by organization, province, data type and deployment.
