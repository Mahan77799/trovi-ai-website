The real dental front-desk problem

Access, attendance and privacy all meet at the phone.

A dental call is rarely just “answer the phone.” The team may need to identify a new patient, protect a booked chair, move an appointment, answer a routine office question or decide whether an urgent concern must reach a person immediately.

01 · NEW PATIENT ACCESS

The first call should not disappear into voicemail.

Trovi can capture who is calling, what type of appointment they are seeking and the approved information needed to move the request toward scheduling or a staff handoff.

02 · BROKEN APPOINTMENTS

A missed chair disrupts more than revenue.

Dental literature consistently treats no-shows as an operational problem that wastes clinical capacity and interrupts workflow. Reminders, confirmations and easier rescheduling are proven parts of the solution.

03 · URGENT CALLS

Urgent needs a route, not an AI diagnosis.

Trovi can follow clinic-approved escalation rules and transfer the caller to the right human path. It should never decide what treatment a patient needs.

Protect the schedule

Make confirmation and rescheduling easier before a chair goes empty.

The ADA notes that dental practices commonly use phone, text or email confirmations. Evidence across healthcare and dentistry supports reminder systems as a way to improve attendance and make cancellations or rescheduling happen sooner.

  • Confirm upcoming appointments using the communication method your clinic supports.
  • Handle reschedule and cancellation requests according to clinic rules.
  • Offer approved next steps or hand the request to staff when availability needs judgement.
  • Keep reminder content limited to the information your privacy policy allows.

Keep the boundary clear

Administrative automation should stop where clinical judgement starts.

A strong dental workflow is defined as much by what it does not answer as by what it does. Trovi can give clinic-approved administrative information and route calls, while symptoms, treatment recommendations, diagnoses and other clinical decisions stay with licensed dental professionals.

Privacy matters: RCDSO guidance says Ontario dentists must protect personal health information under PHIPA. Appointment communications should use consented channels and avoid unnecessary treatment details. Any Trovi deployment for a dental clinic should be configured around the clinic's privacy obligations and data-handling requirements.
Straight answers

What dental clinic owners ask first.

The safest workflow is one that clearly separates administrative access from clinical care.

Can Trovi answer a patient who describes symptoms?

Trovi should not diagnose or recommend treatment. It can follow your clinic's approved instructions, collect limited information needed for routing, and transfer the patient to the appropriate human or emergency path.

Can it book into our practice-management system?

Where a supported integration exists, the workflow can be connected. If not, we build the handoff around the system your clinic already uses instead of claiming an integration that is unavailable.

Can it send appointment reminders?

Yes, if the clinic's workflow and consent process support the chosen channel. Reminder content should be configured to respect privacy requirements and avoid unnecessary personal health information.

Can it answer insurance questions?

It can provide clinic-approved administrative information such as accepted payment methods or general office policies. Benefits interpretation, coverage guarantees and anything requiring insurer verification should be handled through the appropriate system or staff process.
